## 2. Novelty assessment

**Already taken (do not claim):** proverb benchmarks per language (Persian ×3, Italian, Vietnamese, Yemeni, Darija, Nepali*, Ethiopian, Moroccan); multicultural proverb understanding (MAPS); translation-free culturally grounded benchmarks (ADAGE); understanding-vs-pragmatic-use model evaluation in Arabic+English (Kinayat); African-language cultural QA with native-speaker construction and competence controls (Afri-MCQA); broad African LLM benchmarking (AfroBench); "LLM judges are unreliable multilingually" (Fu & Liu); Yoruba proverb prompting with LLM-judged scoring (CG-CoT).
**Still open (verified by absence in the verified set):** (a) **no verified work audits whether automatic metrics / LLM judges validly separate meaning fidelity from pragmatic appropriateness for figurative-cultural content**, across languages — Fu & Liu audit cross-task consistency, not construct validity; Kinayat measures models, not evaluators; (b) **no human-anchored evaluation of Yoruba proverb reasoning exists on arXiv** (CG-CoT is LLM-judged only); (c) tri-lingual (EN/AR/YO) paired designs with shared items are absent from the verified set; (d) recovered ProverbGap artifacts (90 tri-lingual proverbs × 6 models × 2 tasks × 2 strategies, 1,677 successful generations) are a ready-made testbed for (a)+(b) without new inference.

## 3. Recommended research question and method

**Candidate RQ (hypothesis to evaluate, not a conclusion):**
> For source-cleared English, Arabic, and Yoruba proverb items, do existing evaluation approaches validly distinguish **meaning fidelity** (does the output state the proverb's intended meaning?) from **pragmatic appropriateness** (does it identify a correct context of use?), and how sensitive are their conclusions to language, contextual support, lexical shortcuts, contamination, and evaluator choice?

**Direct comparison with prior work:** Kinayat establishes that *models* perform worse on pragmatic use than understanding (−14.07% in AR/EN) — it does not ask whether our *instruments* (BLEU/ChrF/embeddings/LLM-judges) can detect that difference validly, nor whether the instruments behave consistently in Yoruba. Fu & Liu show judges are inconsistent across languages (κ≈0.3) but on generic tasks. VIVID shows judge calibration is feasible (κ=0.792, one language). No verified work combines: tri-lingual items, both constructs, and an evaluator-validity audit. If a reviewer names such a paper, we narrow to the languages/constructs it omits (per the standing invitation for challenge).

**Design (Option 1 — primary): human-anchored evaluator-validity audit on recovered artifacts, no new inference for the core.**
- **Data:** the recovered Generation pilot — 90 tri-lingual proverbs (30/language) × 6 models × 2 tasks (a: literal-meaning explanation; b: cultural variant) × 2 strategies (zs/cot_en); 1,677 successful generations (success rates 34.7–96.9% per model — verified). Annotate a stratified subsample.
- **Primary outcome:** per-language agreement/correlation between human construct ratings and each automatic score (BLEU, chrF, embedding similarity, and — budget-gated — 2–3 LLM judges), for *meaning fidelity* and *pragmatic appropriateness* separately; plus judge-vs-human deltas across languages.
- **H1:** automatic metrics correlate more strongly with meaning fidelity than with pragmatic appropriateness (i.e., they under-detect the pragmatic dimension). **H2:** judge-metric agreement with humans degrades from English → Arabic → Yoruba (direction predicted by Fu & Liu; the study tests it, not assumes it).
- **Statistics:** unit = generation (nested in proverb × model × task); mixed-effects or cluster-bootstrap by proverb; per-language reporting; 95% CIs; no unregistered post-hoc slices.
- **Human annotation:** 2–3 competent raters per language (native/advanced for YO/AR; fluent for EN); existing `annotation_app` (in `proverb_mcq`) reused; dual-annotation on 100% of the pilot subsample, adjudication on disagreements; report Fleiss κ + adjudication log; target κ ≥ 0.6 or label exploratory.
- **Exploratory sizing (honest):** existing per-item human variance is unknown, so power cannot be computed yet. Pilot: 10–15 proverbs/language × 3–4 models × 2 tasks ≈ 120–240 items/language, ×2 raters ≈ 480–960 judgments — labeled **exploratory**; a small variance-estimation batch (30 double-rated items) precedes any confirmatory sizing. A 30–40-item total would be strictly exploratory and must not be generalized across languages.
- **Metric roles:** BLEU/ChrF/embeddings = secondary descriptives only (already computed; e.g., mean BLEU 2.7–4.3, semantic-sim 0.153–0.182 — uninterpretable without this human anchor); LLM judges = secondary, budget-gated (~$1–2), calibrated against the human set before any claim.
- **Controls:** gold-copying already quantified (3.7–11.2% per model — exclude or flag); lexical shortcut via future translation-only condition (Kinayat-style) in a follow-up; contamination via the famous-proverb list; prompt sensitivity via the existing 2×2 task×strategy variation.

**Options 2–3 (lower priority):** (2) Yoruba-focused replication/extension of CG-CoT *with* native-speaker validation — narrower, fully blocked on Yoruba rights, and partially covered by Kinayat's design logic; (3) building another tri-lingual proverb benchmark — dominated by ADAGE/Afri-MCQA/AfroBench prior art and the highest cost; rejected as default. No African-model superiority comparison: no verified matched comparator exists (see §4).

## 5. Go/no-go gates before any run

1. **Rights:** Yoruba source resolved (EMNLP corpus identified + licence, or permission) or annotation restricted to cleared items — but Yoruba is not silently dropped; if unresolved, the study runs EN+AR and reports Yoruba as blocked-by-rights explicitly. Arabic source identified. English scrape documented.
2. **Rubric freeze:** written definitions of meaning fidelity vs pragmatic appropriateness + annotation guidelines + adjudication rule, before any rating.
3. **Annotators:** 2+ competent raters per language recruited; IAA target κ ≥ 0.6 (else exploratory label).
4. **Reproducibility:** input snapshot hash of the selected generations; deterministic scoring scripts (already exist: `docs/decisions/supporting/recompute_checks.py`); cost-capped optional judge run (≤$2, written cap).
5. **Interpretation gate:** predefine that only human-anchored correlations with CIs support claims about metric validity; no model-skill claims from this audit.
