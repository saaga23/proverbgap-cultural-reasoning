## 2. Novelty assessment

**Already taken (do not claim):** proverb benchmarks per language (Persian ×3, Italian, Vietnamese, Yemeni, Darija, Nepali*, Ethiopian, Moroccan); multicultural proverb understanding (MAPS); translation-free culturally grounded benchmarks (ADAGE); understanding-vs-pragmatic-use model evaluation in Arabic+English (Kinayat); African-language cultural QA with native-speaker construction and competence controls (Afri-MCQA); broad African LLM benchmarking (AfroBench); "LLM judges are unreliable multilingually" (Fu & Liu); Yoruba proverb prompting with LLM-judged scoring (CG-CoT).
**Still open (verified by absence in the verified set):** (a) **no verified work audits whether automatic metrics / LLM judges validly separate meaning fidelity from pragmatic appropriateness for figurative-cultural content**, across languages — Fu & Liu audit cross-task consistency, not construct validity; Kinayat measures models, not evaluators; (b) **no human-anchored evaluation of Yoruba proverb reasoning exists on arXiv** (CG-CoT is LLM-judged only); (c) tri-lingual (EN/AR/YO) paired designs with shared items are absent from the verified set; (d) recovered ProverbGap artifacts (90 tri-lingual proverbs × 6 models × 2 tasks × 2 strategies, 1,677 successful generations) are a ready-made testbed for (a)+(b) without new inference.

## 3. Recommended research question and method

**Candidate RQ (hypothesis to evaluate, not a conclusion — canonical wording, 2026-10-26):**
> **Across English, Arabic, and Yoruba proverb-generation outputs, how well do automatic evaluation metrics align with blinded human judgments of meaning fidelity, and how does that alignment vary by language and task?**

Construct definitions: **meaning fidelity** = does the output state the proverb's intended meaning; **pragmatic appropriateness** = does it identify a correct context of use. The verified Task A/B prompts are meaning-side only, so the primary outcome covers meaning fidelity; the pragmatic axis requires the separately gated usage-scenario condition (§5, gate 4) and is otherwise out of scope.

**Direct comparison with prior work:** Kinayat establishes that *models* perform worse on pragmatic use than understanding (−14.07% in AR/EN) — it does not ask whether our *instruments* (BLEU/ChrF/embeddings/LLM-judges) can detect that difference validly, nor whether the instruments behave consistently in Yoruba. Fu & Liu show judges are inconsistent across languages (κ≈0.3) but on generic tasks. VIVID shows judge calibration is feasible (κ=0.792, one language). No verified work combines: tri-lingual items, both constructs, and an evaluator-validity audit. If a reviewer names such a paper, we narrow to the languages/constructs it omits (per the standing invitation for challenge).

**Design (Option 1 — primary): human-anchored evaluator-validity audit on recovered artifacts, no new inference for the core.**
- **Data (prompts verified from the recovered notebook source, 2026-10-26):** the recovered Generation pilot — 90 tri-lingual proverbs (30/language) × 6 models × 2 tasks × 2 strategies; 1,677 successful generations (success rates 34.7–96.9% per model). The **verified prompt design is meaning-side only**: Task A asks for the **literal meaning** in 1–2 sentences (reference = the English translation); Task B asks for a **culturally equivalent English translation/idiom** (reference = the cultural context); strategies are zs vs cot_en. **Neither prompt requests usage scenarios or contextual application.** The notebook also defines a 4-dimension `JUDGE_PROMPT` (accuracy/fluency/cultural-appropriateness/faithfulness, 1–5), but no judge-score columns exist in the recovered CSV — judge outputs were never saved, so judge agreement cannot be assessed from this artifact without a new (budget-gated) judging run.
- **What the existing outputs can and cannot establish:** they support a **meaning-fidelity evaluator-validity audit** (human ratings of literal-meaning and cultural-equivalence outputs vs BLEU/chrF/embeddings), plus the task-A-vs-B contrast as a secondary descriptive axis. They **cannot** support a pragmatic-appropriateness evaluator audit: rating "usage appropriateness" of outputs that never address usage would measure near-universal absence (a floor effect), not instrument validity. A pragmatic axis requires a **new generation condition** (a usage-scenario prompt, Kinayat-style) — a budget-gated extension, explicitly *not* part of the no-new-inference core.
- **Primary outcome (corrected):** per-language agreement/correlation between human **meaning-fidelity** ratings and each automatic score (BLEU, chrF, embedding similarity; LLM judges only after a budget-gated judging run), computed within each task (A and B) separately. Secondary descriptive axis: the A-vs-B contrast (does each metric reward cultural equivalence differently from literal meaning?). Pragmatic appropriateness is **deferred** to the gated extension above.
- **H1 (testable on existing outputs):** automatic metrics' agreement with human meaning-fidelity ratings differs across metrics and across the A/B tasks (i.e., n-gram/embedding metrics may not distinguish a faithful literal explanation from a faithful cultural-equivalent translation). **H2 (descriptive only — see identifiability note):** human–metric agreement varies across languages; a declining EN→AR→YO pattern is *consistent with* Fu & Liu's finding but is **not a controlled cross-language inference** here.
- **Statistics:** unit = generation (nested in proverb × model × task); mixed-effects or cluster-bootstrap by proverb; per-language reporting; 95% CIs; no unregistered post-hoc slices.
- **Human annotation:** two competent raters per language — **six in total across EN/AR/YO** — each rating only their assigned language (native/advanced speakers for YO/AR; fluent for EN); existing `annotation_app` (in `proverb_mcq`) reused; dual-annotation on 100% of the pilot subsample, adjudication on disagreements; report Fleiss κ + adjudication log; target κ ≥ 0.6 or label exploratory. Single rating dimension on existing outputs: **meaning fidelity** (per the prompt-design correction above); a binary "usage scenario mentioned?" meta-flag is optional and cheap. (If a third rater per language were added for agreement checking, rating events would scale by 1.5 — 1,080 for the smaller pilot, 2,160 for the larger — accounted separately from the two-rater baseline below.)
- **Cross-language identifiability (correction):** the 90 items are **different proverbs per language** (30 EN + 30 AR + 30 YO — no parallel/translated pairs exist in the recovered data), and each language uses a different annotator pool. Between-language contrasts (e.g., "agreement is lower in Yoruba") are therefore **confounded by item sets and rater pools and are descriptive only**; the identifiable comparisons are *within-language* (same items, same raters, multiple metrics vs the same human standard). H2 is reported as a descriptive cross-language pattern with these confounds stated; a controlled cross-language claim would require anchor items shared across languages, which the current rights position does not permit.
- **Exploratory sizing (recalculated 2026-10-26 from the actual design formula):** outputs to rate per language = proverbs × models × **2 tasks × 2 strategies** (the earlier 480–960 figure omitted the strategy factor and mixed per-language with totals). With dual annotation (2 raters) and one rating dimension:
  - Smaller pilot: 10 proverbs × 3 models × 2 × 2 = **120 outputs/language → 360 outputs total → 720 rating events** (assuming all rateable). Each rater judges only their own language: 120 outputs ≈ **3–4 hours** at 1.5–2 min per rating.
  - Larger pilot: 15 × 4 × 2 × 2 = **240 outputs/language → 720 outputs total → 1,440 rating events**. Each rater judges 240 outputs ≈ **6–8 hours**.
  - These are planning estimates, not measured annotation times. Only successful generations are rateable, and model success rates were uneven (34.7%–96.9%) — the final sample must be drawn from actual successful outputs under a prespecified missing-data rule, so the totals are upper bounds, not guarantees.
  - Optional: the gated judging run (2–3 LLM judges on the same rated outputs) adds ~360–720 judge calls, not human effort.
  - Labeled **exploratory**; a 30-double-rated-item variance batch precedes any confirmatory sizing. A 30–40-item total would be strictly exploratory and must not be generalized across languages.
- **Metric roles:** BLEU/ChrF/embeddings = secondary descriptives only (already computed; e.g., mean BLEU 2.7–4.3, semantic-sim 0.153–0.182 — uninterpretable without this human anchor); LLM judges = secondary, budget-gated (~$1–2), calibrated against the human set before any claim.
- **Controls:** gold-copying already quantified (3.7–11.2% per model — exclude or flag); lexical shortcut via future translation-only condition (Kinayat-style) in a follow-up; contamination via the famous-proverb list; prompt sensitivity via the existing 2×2 task×strategy variation.

**Options 2–3 (lower priority):** (2) Yoruba-focused replication/extension of CG-CoT *with* native-speaker validation — narrower, fully blocked on Yoruba rights, and partially covered by Kinayat's design logic; (3) building another tri-lingual proverb benchmark — dominated by ADAGE/Afri-MCQA/AfroBench prior art and the highest cost; rejected as default. No African-model superiority comparison: no verified matched comparator exists (see §4).

## 5. Go/no-go gates before any run

1. **Rights:** Yoruba source resolved (EMNLP corpus identified + licence, or permission) or annotation restricted to cleared items — but Yoruba is not silently dropped; if unresolved, the study runs EN+AR and reports Yoruba as blocked-by-rights explicitly. Arabic source identified. English scrape documented.
2. **Rubric freeze:** written definitions of meaning fidelity vs pragmatic appropriateness + annotation guidelines + adjudication rule, before any rating.
3. **Annotators:** 2+ competent raters per language recruited; IAA target κ ≥ 0.6 (else exploratory label).
4. **Reproducibility & scope gate:** input snapshot hash of the selected generations; deterministic scoring scripts (already exist: `docs/decisions/supporting/recompute_checks.py`); cost-capped optional runs (written caps): a judging run (2–3 LLM judges on already-rated outputs, ≤$2) **and, separately, the pragmatic-appropriateness generation condition** (a usage-scenario prompt on the same 90 items) — the latter is the only path to a pragmatic-axis audit, since the verified Task A/B prompts are meaning-side only; no pragmatic claims may be made from the existing outputs.
5. **Interpretation gate:** predefine that only human-anchored correlations with CIs support claims about metric validity; no model-skill claims from this audit.
